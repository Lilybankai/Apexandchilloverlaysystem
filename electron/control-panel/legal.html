YouTube API Services

The Chat and StreamBot features use YouTube API Services. Linking a YouTube account is optional — the rest of the app works without it — and nothing below happens until you link one.

By using those features you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's own handling of your information is governed by the Google Privacy Policy (google.com/policies/privacy), which we ask you to read alongside this one.

What the app accesses through YouTube

When you link an account, you grant the app the youtube.force-ssl permission, and it uses that to:

  • read your channel name and channel ID, so the app can show you which account is linked;
  • find your active live broadcast and the ID of its live chat, so it knows which chat to attach to;
  • read the messages in that live chat while you are streaming — the author's display name, the message text and the badges YouTube supplies (owner, moderator, member) — so the Chat overlay can show them;
  • post messages to that live chat as you, but only for chat commands and announcements you have switched on in StreamBot. It never posts unless you enable it.

It does not access your videos, playlists, subscribers, analytics, watch history or account settings, and it does not upload, edit or delete anything on your channel.

Where that information goes, and how long it is kept

  • Nothing from YouTube is sent to our servers. The app on your PC talks to Google directly. We never see your YouTube data, your tokens or your viewers' messages, and none of it is stored in our database or shared with anyone.
  • Your access tokens are stored only in the app's own folder on your PC, and are used solely to keep the link alive.
  • Viewer chat messages are not stored. They are held in memory only long enough to draw the on-screen chat, are dropped as newer messages arrive, and are never written to disk or uploaded. Nothing read from YouTube API Services is retained beyond the session it was read in.

Removing the link and deleting the data

  • In the app: Settings → Streaming chat → YouTube account → Unlink. This deletes the stored tokens from your PC and stops all access immediately.
  • At Google: you can revoke the app's access to your Google account at any time from the Google security settings page, myaccount.google.com/permissions (also reachable at security.google.com/settings/security/permissions). Revoking there ends the app's access even if the app is not running.
  • Uninstalling the app and deleting its data folder removes the stored tokens along with everything else held locally.
  • Because no YouTube data ever reaches our servers, deleting your Apex account does not need to — and cannot — remove anything at Google.

Who sees what

  • Other signed-in users can see your display name, your leaderboard laps and their driving traces, and the setups you publish with their ratings. That is the point of those features — don't put anything in a display name, setup name or note you wouldn't share.
  • League admins can additionally see your email address, how often you use the app, your feedback, and your subscription status, to run the league and support the app.
  • The race-engineer summary can include the display names of the other drivers in your session (so the answer can name your rival).
